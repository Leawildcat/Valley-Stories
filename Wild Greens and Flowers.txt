## **Prairie Coneflower (JUN–SEP)**  
- Plains/prairie species; not originally native to the Valley  
- Seeds tracked in over generations by humans, horses, wolves, and wind  
- The Valley accepted it because of its bright color and cheerful form  
- Now naturalized in scattered pockets across the Meadow and trail margins  
- Thrives in disturbed soil where horses graze and wolves pass through  
- Appears near the lake approach, along the entrance path, and in sunny clearings  
- Valley’s protected bowl allows it to bloom longer than typical prairie ranges  
- Adds warm yellow and red tones to the Meadow’s summer palette  
- Considered a “welcomed wanderer” — not planted intentionally, but embraced  
- Symbolizes movement, travel, and the living connection between Valley and outside world  
- Blossoms sway in the breeze like small signal flags, marking paths and crossings  
- Part of the Valley’s scent signature in late summer, blending with clover and mint
